# CRM competitor research: deal timelines, activity linking, lost deals, deals table

Research for SimpleCRM's three stated problems: (1) lost deals disappear, (2) activities attach to
company not deal, (3) no per-deal timeline and an unsearchable deals table.

Confidence key: **[docs]** = vendor documentation/changelog fetched directly. **[search]** = search-engine
summary of vendor docs, not independently re-verified by fetching the page. **[3rd-party]** = independent
review/blog, treat as lower confidence, may be stale or wrong in detail.

## 1. Per-deal timeline / activity feed

- **Pipedrive** ([Deal detail view](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/deal-detail-view)): the deal page is a sidebar (deal score, value, contacts, org, custom fields) plus a tabbed "history area" — Notes / Activities / Emails / Files / Documents / Invoices are separate tabs you switch between, not one merged feed. A distinct **Changelog tab** shows dated field-level edits (stage, value, labels, contacts, custom fields) — i.e. Pipedrive splits "what happened" (activities/notes/emails) from "what changed" (changelog) into different tabs rather than one interleaved feed. A **"Focus" panel** surfaces pending activities, draft emails, and pinned notes that need attention — a working-set view layered on top of the historical tabs. The KB article doesn't confirm day-grouping or a planned-vs-done split within the Activities tab itself, but a separate article on [marking activities done](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/activity-marked-as-done-logic) confirms overdue/today/to-do/done are distinct filter states you apply, and [seeing done activities](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-can-i-see-done-activities) requires an explicit `Done = true` filter — so planned vs. past is filter-driven, not two permanently split panes.

- **Attio** ([data model](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/attios-data-model/understanding-attio-data-model), [add record activities](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/records/add-record-activities), [new activity timeline changelog](https://attio.com/changelog/2026/new-activity-timeline)): single **Activity tab** per record, one unified reverse-chronological feed combining task events (overdue/upcoming/completed all in the same stream) and field-change/record history. Filterable by event type, and — a nice touch — **the filter selection is remembered per object type**, so a sales rep's Deal-object filter (e.g. "just meetings + stage changes") persists without re-configuring per record. Composer is inline on the record page itself: click "+ Add meeting" directly on the Activity tab, fill title/summary/date/participants/linked-records in place — no modal, no separate page.

- **HubSpot**: timeline is a single interleaved feed per record (notes, calls, emails, meetings, tasks, property changes), with an inline "log activity" composer bar at the top of the Sales Workspace record view — you pick activity type from a row of icons and it drops straight into the feed. Filterable by activity type via the timeline's filter control.

- **Close**: notably call-centric — the dialer and "Log a Call" live as first-class actions (a dropdown next to the call icon), not buried in a generic "add activity" menu; logging a call inline captures notes + contact + duration in one step. This "the action IS the composer" pattern (log-a-call vs. compose-a-generic-activity-then-pick-type) is worth studying even though Close's timeline is Lead-level, not deal-level.

- **Copper**: Activity Log is explicitly framed around outcome — synced emails/calendar events/other activities are shown as building toward the Won/Lost outcome, which is a nice framing device but not a different information architecture.

- **Freshsales**: separates a **Deal timeline view** — a forecasting-oriented view that segments deals by expected close date across a time axis — from the per-record **activity timeline**, which groups logged activities into tabs by type. These are two different "timeline" concepts under one product name, worth not confusing when researching Freshsales further.

**Takeaway for SimpleCRM**: nobody researched here does true day-grouped chronological virtual scrolling as the headline feature — the differentiator that shows up repeatedly is (a) an **inline composer directly on the record page** (Attio, HubSpot, Close) rather than a modal or separate tab, and (b) **type filtering with tabs or filter chips**, not date-range filtering. Pipedrive's split between "Activities" and "Changelog" tabs is worth copying: stage/field edits are structurally different from human-authored notes/calls/emails and reading them interleaved gets noisy.

## 2. Activity↔deal↔company↔contact linking model — the data-model question

Three distinct architectures showed up:

**A. Single polymorphic parent (Salesforce, Pipedrive).** An activity has exactly one "primary" relation to a deal/opportunity/org (Salesforce: `WhatId`, polymorphic FK to Account/Opportunity/Case/custom object) plus one "who" relation to a person (Salesforce: `WhoId`, polymorphic FK to Contact/Lead) — [Salesforce data model docs](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/data-models/guide/tasks-events.html), [WhoId/WhatId explainer](https://www.apexhours.com/understanding-whatid-and-whoid-in-salesforce/). Pipedrive is structurally identical in spirit: **an activity can only have one linked lead/deal/org, but unlimited linked people** ([confirmed via Pipedrive KB search results](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-can-i-link-my-activities-with-multiple-contact-persons)). Crucially, Pipedrive's org (company) detail view does **not** store a multi-link on the activity — it computes the rollup by **traversal**: "any notes or activities linked to any people, leads, deals or projects related to that organization" get displayed on the org page, but an activity linked directly to the org is *not* automatically linked to its deals. This is the cleanest evidence that even a company-timeline rollup doesn't require many-to-many storage — a query that joins through deal→company (or person→company) is enough.

**B. True many-to-many join (HubSpot).** Every activity ("engagement") can be associated with an arbitrary number of contacts/companies/deals/tickets/custom objects via an associations table. HubSpot auto-populates sensible defaults per source object (e.g., an activity logged on a Deal auto-associates that deal's primary Company + the 5 most recent open deals on that company + any contacts emailed/called), fully overridable per-activity via an "associations" dropdown. Critically, **HubSpot separates "is this activity associated with record X" from "should it display on record X's timeline"** — there's a per-record checkbox to opt a related record's already-logged activities into *this* record's timeline, meaning storage is many-to-many but display is still curated, avoiding "every activity from every related contact drowns my deal's history." ([knowledge.hubspot.com/records/associate-activities-with-records](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/associate-activities-with-records))

**C. Flexible record-reference relational graph (Attio).** Attio's general `record-reference` attribute type has an `is_multiselect` flag, so any attribute can be one-to-one, one-to-many, or many-to-many, and standard cross-object links (Company↔People via `team`, People↔Deals via `associated_deals`) are implemented as **paired bidirectional attributes** — writing one side auto-updates the other ([docs.attio.com/rest-api/attribute-types/attribute-types-record-reference](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/attribute-types/attribute-types-record-reference)). On top of that generic graph, Activities (meetings etc.) carry their own explicit `linked records` field so one logged interaction can be attached to a deal, a company, and several people simultaneously, each of which independently shows it on their own Activity tab.

**Where SimpleCRM sits today vs. these**: your Activity is presumably FK'd to Company only, with no Deal FK at all — closer to "no parent" than even the weakest of the above models. All three architectures above at minimum let an activity reach a deal.

## 3. Lost / failed deals

- **Pipedrive**: no graveyard column on the board — [confirmed via the "filtering for won/lost/deleted deals" article](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/filtering-for-my-won-lost-or-deleted-deals): "won, lost, and deleted deals are hidden from the main pipeline board by default and must be accessed through the filtering feature," shared identically between board and list view. Lost-reason capture: [freeform by default](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/lost-reasons); an admin can flip on **predefined dropdown reasons** (up to 100) which still allow a free-text comment alongside the picked reason — i.e. "dropdown + optional free text," not either/or. Reporting: dedicated Insights "deal performance" reports can filter/group by lost reason for trend analysis. Reopening a genuinely lost (not deleted) deal isn't documented cleanly in official docs — Pipedrive's own community has open threads asking for automated "reopen after N months" behavior, suggesting reopening today is a manual drag/filter action, not a one-click "resurrect" feature. Separately, **Rotting** ([support.pipedrive.com/en/article/the-rotting-feature](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/the-rotting-feature)) is Pipedrive's stale-deal warning: per-pipeline-stage configurable threshold (days), deals idle past it get a colored border/clock icon directly on the kanban card, timer resets on any deal update (activity done, stage move, email sent/received). This is a genuinely well-designed, low-friction feature — pure UI + one config number per stage, no new entities.

- **HubSpot**: `Closed Lost Reason` ships as **free text by default** — somewhat surprising for the market leader — and becomes a structured dropdown only if an admin customizes the property and marks it required at the Closed Lost stage ([community threads](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/Tips-Tricks-Best-Practices/How-to-create-a-dropdown-for-closed-lost-reason/m-p/744033), [umbrex walkthrough](https://umbrex.com/resources/how-to-set-up-and-use-hubspot-crm/customizing-the-closed-lost-reason-property/)). Board view has a one-click "Closed Lost" filter chip.

- **Salesforce**: `Reason Lost` is an optional field, not required out of the box — needs an explicit validation rule to enforce ([Data Quality Helper use case](https://www.passagetechnology.com/data-quality-helper-use-cases/ensuring-reasons-lost-are-included-on-closed-lost-opportunities-in-salesforce)). Best-practice guidance is notably opinionated against blindly "reopening": [Letterdrop's writeup](https://letterdrop.com/blog/reopen-closed-lost-salesforce) recommends **cloning** a closed-lost opportunity into a new one rather than reopening the original, specifically to avoid corrupting historical win-rate/forecast reporting and sales-cycle-length metrics. This is a real trade-off SimpleCRM should think about: reopen-in-place is simpler UX but pollutes stage-duration and win-rate analytics; clone-forward keeps the loss reason as a permanent record and starts a clean pipeline entry.

- **Copper**: dragging a card to the Lost column **prompts** a Loss Reason pick from a configurable, renameable, reorderable list (custom reasons allowed), feeding a built-in "Loss Reasons" report ([support.copper.com/en/articles/8823335](https://support.copper.com/en/articles/8823335-understand-and-customize-loss-reasons-field)) — closest in spirit to what SimpleCRM already has (a `lost_reason` field), just packaged with a stock report.

- **Zoho CRM**: `Reason for Loss` recently changed from free text to a **system-defined picklist** (per Zoho's own community post), and — notably — a deal **can be dragged straight back** from Closed Lost to Won/an open stage; no special restore flow, reopening is just a normal stage move. Simplest reopen model of everything reviewed.

- **Freshsales**: ships a sample **"Lost deals" custom view** (filter: stage = Lost) side-by-side with Open/My/Territory/Recent/Cold/Hot/Won sample views — reinforces that "lost deals" across nearly every vendor here is a **saved filter, not a special screen or archive**.

**Cross-vendor pattern**: not one of the 6 vendors with confirmed detail here uses a permanent "graveyard" board column for lost deals — universally it's a filter/saved-view, with the board/list defaulting to open deals only. Rotting/stale-deal warnings (only confirmed for Pipedrive) are a distinct, additive feature from "lost" handling, not related to it.

## 4. Searchable / filterable deals table

- **Pipedrive**: [List view](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/list-view) + [customizing columns](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/customizing-the-columns-in-the-list-view) (gear icon, any deal/person/org field can be a column, drag to reorder) + [quick filters](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/quick-filters) (fast single-condition filters applied directly from list/board) + [advanced filters](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/filtering) (AND/OR condition groups) + **saved filters** that can optionally bundle a specific column set and be shared org-wide + [bulk edit](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/bulk-editing-and-filtering) from the list view. Filters are shared identically between board and list view — one filter engine, two renderers.

- **HubSpot**: board and table view are two renderings of the same filtered/sorted deal set; "Edit columns" on both; dedicated Forecast view (Sales Hub Pro+) rolls up by stage-probability × expected close date × owner.

- **Zoho CRM**: predefined views out of the box (All Locked Deals, Closing This Month, My Deals) + user Custom Views with **up to 25 filter criteria**, reorderable/removable columns, and list-view bulk actions (owner change, bulk delete, mass email).

- **Freshsales**: 4 built-in views (Open/My/My Territory/Recent) + sample custom views (Lost/Cold/Hot/Won) that are just templates for the general saved-view mechanism — users edit/delete/clone them freely, and views are shareable subject to permissions.

- **Attio / Close**: both center on **saved dynamic views/segments as the primary object**, not a single canonical "the deals table" — arguably the most modern approach and the best one for a small, opinionated team since there's no separate "board vs. list vs. forecast" mode-switching, just different views of the same filtered set.

**Takeaway for SimpleCRM**: the deals list described in the brief (fixed columns: name, company, value, stage, owner, close date, status) is behind every vendor surveyed in exactly the same way — none of them ship a fixed table with no per-user column/filter control. The floor for "table view" credibility in this market is: configurable columns + saved filters + quick filter chips + bulk edit. None of that requires exotic UI — it's the single highest-leverage, lowest-risk item in this whole research task.

## 5. Notable adjacent features worth stealing

- **Pipedrive Rotting** — per-stage configurable "days idle" threshold, red border + clock icon on the card, no new entities needed (just a per-stage integer config + a computed `days_since_last_update`). Directly solves "deals silently going stale," which is adjacent to (arguably a superset of) the lost-deals visibility problem — a rotting deal today might become a silently-lost deal tomorrow if nobody notices.
- **Attio's per-object-remembered activity filter** — small but real UX polish: the timeline filter choice (e.g. "just calls + meetings") persists per object type across sessions, so a rep doesn't re-filter every time they open a deal.
- **HubSpot's "roll up into this record's timeline" checkbox** — the key insight that makes many-to-many association safe: storage can be permissive (associate broadly) while display stays curated (a company shouldn't drown in every contact's every email by default).
- **Close's "the action is the composer"** pattern for calls — a call-log button that inlines notes+duration+contact in one step rather than "add activity → pick type → fill form."
- **Zoho's frictionless reopen** — dragging Closed Lost straight back to an open stage, no separate "restore" concept to learn.
- **Salesforce's clone-instead-of-reopen best practice** — worth adopting as *guidance*, not necessarily as a forced UX: if SimpleCRM lets a lost deal be reopened in-place, it should recompute/flag that stage-duration and win-rate numbers for that deal are now suspect, or offer "reopen" vs. "clone as new deal, keep this one as historical record" as two distinct actions.

## What SimpleCRM should copy, and what to skip

### Problem 1 — lost deals disappear
**Copy:** treat "lost" as a **filter/saved-view state, never a special screen** — this is the unanimous pattern (Pipedrive, HubSpot, Zoho, Freshsales, Copper all do this). Concretely: default deals list/board excludes Won/Lost; add a one-click "Lost" quick-filter chip (and a "Won" one) next to the default view, so nothing is deleted or archived, it's one click away. Add a **Rotting-style stale-deal indicator**: per-stage or global "days without activity" threshold, visual flag on the deal card/row. This is cheap (no new tables, just a config value + a computed field) and it's the mechanism that actually *prevents* deals from silently dying, which is more valuable than good post-mortem tooling on deals that already died.
**Also copy:** lost-reason as **dropdown with an "Other" free-text fallback** (Pipedrive/Copper/Zoho pattern) — you already have `lost_reason`; check whether it's currently free text or a picklist and consider Copper's model (admin-configurable, reorderable reason list + custom entries) plus a trivial "lost reasons breakdown" report (count/value grouped by reason) — this is a reporting query, not new storage.
**Decide, don't default:** reopening a lost deal. Zoho's frictionless drag-back-to-open-stage is the simplest UX; Salesforce's clone-instead-of-reopen protects win-rate/cycle-time metrics. Given SimpleCRM's scale (small team, metrics probably not load-bearing yet), frictionless reopen (Zoho-style) is the pragmatic choice — just make sure `lost_reason`/`lost_at` get cleared or archived (not silently overwritten) so the loss history isn't lost a second time.
**Skip:** a permanent kanban "graveyard" column — nobody does this, and it clutters the primary working view every vendor is careful to keep clean.
**Migration:** none required beyond what you already have, *if* `lost_reason` is already a column — only a migration if you want to convert it to an enum/picklist (Postgres native enum → needs the `ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE` outside-transaction pattern your CLAUDE.md already flags) or add a `stale_since`/`stage_entered_at` timestamp for rotting (additive nullable column, low risk).

### Problem 2 — activities attached to company, not deal
This is the one genuine data-model decision. Two real options, in order of recommendation:

**Option A (recommended first step) — Pipedrive/Salesforce-style single additive parent.** Add a nullable `deal_id` FK directly to the existing `activities` table (alongside the current `company_id`/`contact_id`). An activity created from within a deal's page gets `deal_id` set (and can still carry the inherited `company_id`). Company timeline keeps querying by `company_id` exactly as today; the new per-deal timeline queries `WHERE deal_id = :id`. This mirrors what the majority of vendors surveyed actually do (Salesforce's `WhatId`, Pipedrive's single deal/lead/org link) — it is not the most flexible model in this survey, but it is what fixes the *stated* problem with a one-column additive migration, no join table, no rewrite of existing activity-rendering code paths (they still work by company_id), and no risk to existing data.
*Trade-off*: an activity can't cleanly belong to two deals at once, and there's no automatic company↔deal rollup — if you want a company's timeline to show its deals' activities too, that's a UNION query (`company_id = X OR deal_id IN (deals of X)`), computed at read time exactly like Pipedrive's org-view traversal, not stored.
**Migration required**: yes — one Alembic migration, `ALTER TABLE activities ADD COLUMN deal_id INTEGER NULL REFERENCES deals(id)`. Low risk, backward compatible, no backfill required (existing rows just have `deal_id = NULL`).

**Option B (later, if flexibility becomes a real need) — HubSpot-style many-to-many join table.** New `activity_links(activity_id, entity_type, entity_id)` table; an activity can attach to any combination of company/contact/deal. Add a "roll up to this record" boolean per link (HubSpot's insight) so a company doesn't drown in every one of its deals' minutiae by default. This is the Attio/HubSpot-grade model — genuinely more correct for a world with multi-deal companies or activities that legitimately span two deals (e.g., a renewal call that touches both an expiring and a new deal) — but it's a bigger lift: new table, migration of every existing activity row into the join table (`company_id` → one row in `activity_links`), and every place in the codebase that currently reads `activity.company_id` needs to become a join query.
**Migration required**: yes, and materially larger than Option A — new table + backfill of all existing rows + rewritten read paths.

**Recommendation**: ship Option A now. It directly and cheaply fixes the stated problem ("no per-deal history") without a schema overhaul, and matches what most of the vendors surveyed actually ship (single-parent activities are the norm, not the exception — true many-to-many is a HubSpot/Attio-tier investment). Revisit Option B only if real usage shows activities routinely needing to span multiple deals.

### Problem 3 — no per-deal timeline, table not searchable/filterable
**Copy for the timeline:** an Activity tab on the deal page with (a) an **inline composer at the top** — not a modal, not a separate "add activity" page (Attio/HubSpot/Close pattern) — and (b) **type filter chips** (call/email/note/meeting/stage-change), remembered per-session the way Attio does. Consider Pipedrive's split of "activities/notes" vs. "changelog" (field/stage edits) into two views/tabs rather than interleaving system-generated diffs with human-authored notes — it keeps the human narrative readable.
**Copy for the table:** this is the highest-leverage, lowest-risk item in the whole report — none of the 9 vendors ship a fixed, non-configurable deals table. Add: (1) a column picker (gear icon, Pipedrive/HubSpot pattern), (2) quick-filter chips for the obvious segments (mine/team, open/won/lost, stale), (3) a proper filter builder (field + operator + value, AND/OR groups a stretch goal — even single-condition "quick filters" get you most of the value per Pipedrive's own two-tier model), (4) bulk actions (bulk owner reassignment, bulk stage move, bulk delete) which every vendor surveyed supports from the list view, (5) full-text search across deal name/company/contact.
**Skip for now:** a forecast view and a calendar view (HubSpot/Salesforce-tier features) — not asked for, not needed at 1–10 seats, and meaningfully more work (probability-weighting, time-bucketing) than the table/timeline fixes above.
**Migration for saved views**: if you want filters/columns to persist per user (recommended — Freshsales/Zoho/Pipedrive all persist them), that needs a new `saved_views` table (`user_id, entity_type, filters_json, columns_json, is_shared`). To avoid a migration in the first cut, ship it client-side (localStorage) and only add server-side persistence if users want views shared across devices/teammates — Pipedrive's own "shared filter" is the case where you'd need it server-side (so a colleague sees the same saved view), which is likely valuable at even 1–10 seats since the whole point is a shared sales team.
